## #who
**When:** with the source's information at hand.
**Do:** write down who the source is, how they know what they say (saw it, were told, hold the document) and what they gain if it is published.
**Links:** [[verify-a-source#anonymous]]

## #anonymous
**When:** the source's role is clear.
**Do:** note whether the source asks not to be named.
**If:** the source is anonymous → [[double-confirm-an-anonymous-source]]
**Because:** readers can't judge an anonymous source, so the newsroom must have judged it for them.
**Links:** [[verify-a-source#records]]

## #records
**When:** before writing.
**Do:** check every fact against a document or a record (company registry, court file, official data) and mark each one confirmed (keep it), partial (attribute it: say who claims it) or unconfirmed (cut it). Two sources who heard it from the same place are one source. A fact whose only source gains from publishing it (a competitor, a rival) needs a record, or it is cut.
**Because:** what isn't confirmed is attributed or left out.
**Links:** [[verify-a-source#right_of_reply]]

## #right_of_reply
**When:** before publishing.
**Do:** list every person and company the story makes a claim about and draft the questions for each one, with a deadline to answer (for example 48 hours before publication, as a date and time).
**Links:** [[verify-a-source#after]]

## #after
**When:** the story is out and a fact turns out to be wrong.
**Do:** correct it openly.
**Calls:** [[publish-a-correction-note]]
