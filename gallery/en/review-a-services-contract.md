## #parties_and_scope
**When:** with the contract at hand.
**Do:** summarise in five lines who provides what to whom, for how long, for how much, and how either side can end it. Say which annexes are missing.
**Because:** half of the problems in a services contract are a scope nobody wrote down.
**Links:** [[review-a-services-contract#liability]]

## #liability
**When:** the scope is clear.
**Do:** check the liability cap, the indemnities and the exclusions against the firm's checklist (examples to replace with yours: a cap of twelve months of fees, no cap for breaches of confidentiality). Quote the clause number of every deviation.
**Because:** the cap decides what the client loses if the provider fails.
**Links:** [[review-a-services-contract#personal_data]]

## #personal_data
**When:** reading what the provider will receive or access.
**Do:** note whether the provider will process personal data of the client's customers or staff (names, emails, ID numbers, call recordings).
**If:** the provider processes personal data → [[add-a-data-protection-clause]]
**Because:** processing personal data on someone else's behalf needs its own clause, and most services templates lack it.
**Links:** [[review-a-services-contract#comments]]

## #comments
**When:** every clause is checked.
**Do:** write the comments as a table: clause, what it says, the risk, the proposed wording. Leave the decision to sign to the person.
**Because:** the lawyer signs off; the table is what the client reads.
