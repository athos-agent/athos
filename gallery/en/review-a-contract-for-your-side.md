## #read_side
**When:** a contract arrives with the side the person acts for.
**Do:** note the side and review every clause from it. Ask whether the contract holds client data that should not leave the computer; if it does, anonymise names and amounts first or use a local model.
**Because:** the same clause is good for one side and bad for the other; professional secrecy is the person's.
**Links:** [[review-a-contract-for-your-side#checklist]]

## #checklist
**When:** reading the contract.
**Do:** check, quoting the clause number: parties, scope, term, price and payment; governing law and jurisdiction (say which is missing); deposit or security and its return; early termination and notice for both sides (flag it when one-sided); days to pay and late interest; liability capped, never unlimited; automatic renewal and assignment. Replace this list with your own checklist.
**If:** one side will process personal data of the other's customers or staff → [[add-a-data-protection-clause]]
**Because:** without a clause number a comment cannot be discussed.
**Links:** [[review-a-contract-for-your-side#table]]

## #table
**When:** every point is checked.
**Do:** write a table: clause, issue, proposed wording, priority (must / should / minor). A missing governing-law clause is a row of its own, priority must.
**Because:** the table is what the client reads; a contract silent on governing law is a risk in itself.
**Links:** [[review-a-contract-for-your-side#push_first]]

## #push_first
**When:** the table is done.
**Do:** end with a line `Push first:` naming the three clauses to negotiate first, and say the decision stays with the client. Never present the proposal as final.
**Because:** the professional judgement, and the responsibility, belong to whoever signs the review.
